----------------------------------------------------------------

Baselines are identical across all three runs (they're computed once per

  - Random baseline:    0.034394
  - BM25 baseline:      0.070867
  - Semantic baseline:  0.079316

| Run           | Delta vs Random | Delta vs BM25 baseline | Delta vs Semantic baseline |
|----------------|------------------:|--------------------------:|------------------------------:|
| Semantic-only  | +0.027039         | -0.009434                  | -0.017883                      |

| Hybrid RRF     | +0.038704         | +0.002232                  | -0.006218                      |

Key read:
  - Every run beats random by a wide margin — all three retrieval strategies
    are meaningfully better than showing arbitrary items.
  - NONE of the three runs beat the semantic baseline outright. The gap to
    the semantic baseline shrinks as you move from semantic-only (-0.0179)
    to BM25-only (-0.0080) to hybrid (-0.0062) — hybrid gets closest, but
    still falls short of that ceiling.
  - Only BM25-only and Hybrid RRF beat the BM25 baseline (by a small margin
    each: +0.0004 and +0.0022 respectively). Semantic-only actually
    underperforms the BM25 baseline by -0.0094 — worth noting, since a pure
    semantic *submission* scoring below the semantic *baseline* by such a
    wide margin (-0.0179) suggests the semantic-only run's slate quality was
    materially worse than whatever reference ranking the baseline uses, not
    just noise.

3. USER ACTION BREAKDOWN
----------------------------------------------------------------

| Action    | Semantic-only | BM25-only | Hybrid RRF |
|-----------|---------------:|-----------:|-----------:|
| seen      | 237,570        | 232,660    | 231,528    |
| click     | 22,444         | 23,155     | 23,489     |
| cart      | 18,311         | 20,409     | 20,817     |
| purchase  | 9,675          | 11,776     | 12,166     |

All three sum to 288,000 served impressions.

Observations:
  - Purchase count climbs steadily: Semantic-only (9,675) -> BM25-only
    (11,776) -> Hybrid (12,166). Hybrid drives ~25.8% more purchases than
    semantic-only and ~3.3% more than BM25-only.
  - Cart adds follow the same pattern: Hybrid (20,817) is the highest,
    ~13.7% above semantic-only and ~2.0% above BM25-only.
  - Click counts are close across all three (22.4K-23.5K), with hybrid
    slightly ahead — most of hybrid's advantage over BM25-only shows up
    further down the funnel (cart/purchase), not just at click.
  - "Seen" count is lowest for hybrid, meaning hybrid converts a larger
    share of impressions into an action rather than a no-action "seen" —
    consistent with it having the best overall expected value.

Observations:
  - BM25-only concentrates purchases on fewer unique items (1,883) than
    semantic-only (1,986) but with a notably higher purchase reach rate
    (67.32% vs 62.20%) — BM25's narrower candidate set converts more
    efficiently per unique item shown.
  - Hybrid has the best of both worlds here: highest purchase reach rate
    (67.39%, edging out BM25-only) AND the most unique items driving
    purchases (2,116) — it's not just narrowly optimizing for a small set
    of "safe" items, it's finding a broader set of purchase-worthy items
    while still converting them well.

----------------------------------------------------------------
7. TIMING NOTE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Server-side validation/simulation time increased substantially across the
three runs: 260.9s (semantic-only) -> 320.4s (BM25-only) -> 738.9s (hybrid).
This is expected — hybrid RRF does more work per query (candidate retrieval
across two channels plus a quality re-rank), so both the client-side build
and the server-side simulation take longer. This is a runtime cost, not a
quality signal — it doesn't affect the expected value comparison above.


----------------------------------------------------------------
8. BOTTOM LINE
----------------------------------------------------------------

  - Hybrid RRF (BM25 + Semantic + Quality) is the best-performing strategy
    of the three tested in Round 1, both on expected value per impression
    and on downstream actions (clicks, carts, purchases).
  - BM25-only is a strong second — it essentially matches its own baseline
    and clearly beats semantic-only.
  - Semantic-only underperformed both other strategies and even its own
    baseline by a wide margin, suggesting the pure top-20 semantic slate
    build (cosine similarity only, no lexical or quality signal) leaves
    meaningful value on the table compared to what a well-tuned semantic
    baseline achieves.
  - None of the three submissions beat the semantic baseline outright,
    which may be worth investigating further in Round 2 (e.g. via the
    LambdaMART reranker) — closing that remaining ~0.006-0.018 gap is the
    next opportunity.
